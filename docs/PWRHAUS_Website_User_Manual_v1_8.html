1. System Overview

The PWRHaus Golf Society website is a marketing and lead-capture site for a co-ed golf society operating in Fort Lauderdale and Miami. It presents the society's events, membership tiers, lessons, corporate offerings and sponsorship, captures leads from every page, and gives you — the owner — a private dashboard to manage the parts of the site that change most often.

1.1 What the Site Does

CapabilityWhat It Means for You
Public marketing siteSeven public pages — Home, Events, Membership, Lessons, Corporate, Sponsors, About — each with a full-width photo or video hero.
Lead captureEvery page has a capture form, plus a site-wide welcome gate on first visit. Each submission is saved to your database and pushed to GoHighLevel.
Events managementYou create, edit, publish, reorder and delete events yourself. Each published event also gets its own page.
Page content editingYou can change the headline text — and the photo — at the top of every public page without a developer.
CRM (leads)A read-only view of everyone who has submitted a form on your site, including their full submission history.
Merchandise Test ModeA storefront driven by Printify with Stripe checkout. Currently in test mode — see Section 11.

1.3 How a Change Goes Live

This is the single most useful thing to understand about your site. The public website is static — it is built into fixed pages rather than looking things up as each visitor arrives. That is what makes it fast and strong for search engines. The trade-off is that a change in the dashboard doesn't appear publicly until the site rebuilds.

You click Save
Your change is stored immediately. The dashboard shows it right away.
Publishing starts
The dashboard automatically tells Netlify to rebuild. You'll see "Publishing… live in ~30s".
Netlify rebuilds
Netlify pulls the newest events and page content and regenerates every page.
Live
Typically 30–60 seconds after you saved, the public site shows your change.
NoteIf you save something and the public page still looks the same, wait a minute and refresh. That is almost always all it needs.

3. Signing In to Your Dashboard

3.1 Where to Go

Your dashboard lives at:

https://pwrhaus.netlify.app/admin/
💡 TipBookmark this. Note the trailing slash — /admin/ — it matters.

3.2 Signing In

  1. Go to https://pwrhaus.netlify.app/admin/
  2. Enter the email address and password you were given
  3. Click Sign in

You'll land on the dashboard with your events listed. Your session is remembered, so you usually won't have to sign in again on the same device.

NoteOnly approved accounts can reach the dashboard. There is no "create account" option, on purpose — that's a security measure protecting your lead list.

3.3 Two-Step Verification New

Signing in now takes a second step: a six-digit code from an authenticator app on your phone. Your password alone is no longer enough to open the dashboard — which matters, because the dashboard holds your entire lead list.

Setting it up (once):

  1. Install an authenticator app on your phone if you don't have one — Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator and 1Password all work
  2. Sign in to the dashboard as usual. You'll see “Secure your dashboard”
  3. Tap Set up authenticator — a QR code appears
  4. In your authenticator app, add a new account and scan that QR code
  5. Type the six-digit code the app shows into Verification code, then tap Verify and continue

Every time you sign in after that: enter your email and password, then open your authenticator app and type the current six-digit code.

💡 The code changes every 30 secondsIf it's about to roll over, wait for the fresh one rather than rushing — a code typed as it expires will be rejected.
⚠ If you lose or replace your phoneYou will not be able to finish signing in, and there is no self-service way to fix it yet — contact your developer, who can clear it so you can set it up again on the new phone. This takes them a couple of minutes.
⚠ Do not lose your authenticatorIf you replace or wipe your phone without moving your authenticator across, you will not be able to sign in and your developer will have to reset it for you. When you set it up, either save the backup text code shown under the QR code somewhere safe, or use an authenticator that syncs across your devices (1Password and Google Authenticator both do).

3.4 Install It on Your Phone

The dashboard can be installed like an app, so it sits on your home screen with the PWRHaus mark and opens full-screen.

  • iPhone / iPad: open the dashboard in Safari → tap the Share button → Add to Home Screen
  • Android: open it in Chrome → tap the three-dot menu → Install app (or use the Install app link in the dashboard sidebar)

3.5 Signing Out

Click Sign out — bottom of the sidebar on a computer, top-right on a phone.

4. Managing Events

4.1 The Events Screen

This is what you see when you sign in. Across the top are four counts:

TileMeaning
UpcomingEvents whose date is still in the future — published or not.
PublishedEvents currently visible on the public website.
DraftEvents you've created but not published. Nobody outside can see these.
RegistrationsShows "—" for now. Reserved for when ticketing is added.

Below the counts, each event appears as a card with its photo, name, city, date, venue, price, capacity, and a Published or Draft badge.

4.2 Add an Event

  1. Click New event (top right, or the New tab on a phone)
  2. Type the Name — the Slug (web address) fills in automatically as you type
  3. Fill in City, Venue, and Date & time
  4. Enter Price in dollars (enter 0 for a free event) and Capacity
  5. Write a Summary — one or two lines, shown on the events list
  6. Write the Body — the longer description on the event's own page
  7. Add a Registration URL if you're using an outside ticketing link (optional)
  8. Click Image and choose a photo from your device — you'll see a preview
  9. Write Image alt text — a short description of the photo (required)
  10. Tick Published when you're ready for it to be public, then click Save event
💡 TipLeave Published unticked to save a draft. Nothing appears publicly until you tick it — the safe way to prepare an event in advance.
Why alt text?It describes the photo for visually impaired visitors and for search engines. One plain sentence is perfect — "Golfers walking together across a green course."
About the web addressThe Slug (web address) box is the end of the link guests see — pwrhausgolfsociety.com/events/fall-founder-scramble/. It is built from the event name for you, using lowercase letters, numbers and hyphens only. You can edit it, but once you have shared the link anywhere — email, Instagram, a text — changing it breaks that link. Set it before you publish, then leave it alone.

4.3 Edit an Event

  1. Find the event card and click Edit
  2. Change whatever you need — every field is editable
  3. Click Save event

Leave the Image field alone if you're happy with the current photo — it stays as it is.

4.4 Publish / Unpublish

Each card has a button reading Publish or Unpublish depending on its current state. Click it to flip.

  • Publish — the event appears on the Events page and gets its own page.
  • Unpublish — it disappears from the public site but is kept safely as a draft.
💡 TipUnpublishing is the gentle alternative to deleting. Use it for an event that's finished, postponed, or not ready.

4.5 Reorder Events

The order of your event cards is the order visitors see. Two ways to change it:

  • Drag and drop — grab a card and move it up or down.
  • Arrow buttons — click ↑ or ↓ on a card. Easier on a phone, and works with a keyboard.

4.6 Duplicate an Event

Click Duplicate to copy an event. The copy is created as an unpublished draft named "… (copy)". Ideal for a recurring event — duplicate it, change the date, publish.

4.7 Delete an Event

  1. Click Delete on the event card
  2. Confirm when asked
⚠ PermanentDeleting cannot be undone. If you only want it off the website, use Unpublish instead.

4.8 Photo Tips

  • Landscape (wider than tall) photos work best on event cards.
  • Photos taken on a modern phone are more than large enough.
  • Very large files make pages slower to load — if a photo is over about 3 MB, resize it first.
  • Uploaded photos are stored automatically. You don't need to name or organise them.

Each hero has three text fields:

FieldWhat It Is

LeadThe sentence or two beneath the headline. Required.
NoteThe buttons in a hero, and the rest of each page's content, are fixed — your developer changes those. This screen is for the headline area.

5.2 Edit a Page Hero

  1. Click Site Content in the sidebar
  2. Choose the page you want from the list
  3. Edit the Eyebrow, Heading and Lead
  4. Click Save page
  5. Wait about 30–60 seconds, then refresh the public page

5.3 Replacing a Hero Photo

The photos and videos already on your pages are part of the website and look after themselves — you don't need to touch them. If you do want to swap a photo, the option is tucked away so it isn't in your way day to day.

  1. In Site Content, choose the page
  2. Click Replace hero media (optional) to expand it
  3. Click Hero image and choose a photo — a preview appears
  4. Adjust Focal position if the important part of the photo is off-centre (see 5.4)
  5. Click Save page
Home and Events pagesThese two use background videos rather than photos. You'll see video and poster path fields instead of an upload button — leave those alone unless your developer has given you a new file name.

5.4 Focal Position

A hero photo is cropped to fit a wide, short banner, so part of it is always cut off. Focal position tells the site which part to keep in view.

It's two percentages — left-to-right, then top-to-bottom:

ValueResult
50% 50%Dead centre. The default.
50% 30%Shows more of the top — good when faces or sky are high in the frame.
50% 70%Shows more of the bottom.
💡 TipIf a new photo looks like it's cutting off someone's head, lower the second number and save again. Adjust in steps of 10 until it looks right.

6.2 Search & Filter

  • Capture period — choose Today, This week, This month, This quarter, or All time to review when the lead was captured.
  • Custom range — choose Custom range, enter the From and To dates, then click Apply dates. Both dates are included.
  • Search box — type part of a name or email; the list narrows as you type.
  • Tier filter — show only Free, Member, or Inner circle.
  • Source filter — show only people who came from a particular form.

You can combine the capture-period filter with search, tier, and source. The filters apply across the entire lead database, not only the 100 contacts currently visible.

💡 ExampleTo review leads captured during a specific campaign month, choose Custom range, enter the first and last day of the month, and click Apply dates. Use All time after the GHL import to confirm the full contact count.
💡 TipAvoid typing % or _ in the search box — they're treated as wildcards and return odd results. Plain words work best.

6.3 Lead Detail

  • View in GHL — opens this person in GoHighLevel
💡 Why the inquiry history mattersSomeone who filled in a free-profile form in September and a sponsorship form in November is a warm lead. That trail is exactly what this panel shows you.

Press Escape or click Close to dismiss the panel.

7. Selling Event Tickets Updated

Your site can now sell tickets to your events directly, with card payments handled by Stripe. This replaces selling tickets through WIX.

⚠ Test Mode — Not Live YetTicketing is currently connected to Stripe in test mode, so no real card can be charged and no real money moves. Everything is built and waiting — switching it on for real sales is a deliberate step (see 7.7).

7.1 How It Works

Buyer picks tickets
On your event page they enter their name, email, and how many tickets they want.
They pay
They are taken to Stripe’s secure checkout — card, Apple Pay or Google Pay. You never handle card details.
Tickets are issued
One ticket per seat, each with its own ticket number. The buyer’s own seat is assigned to them automatically.
They add their guests
They get a private link to name the people joining them. Each guest gets their own ticket — and lands in your CRM.
💡 Why guests are added afterwardsAsking for three colleagues’ email addresses before payment is where group bookings get abandoned. Buyers pay in seconds, then fill in their guests afterwards when they have the details to hand — which also means the addresses you get are accurate.

7.2 Turning Sales On for an Event

Ticket sales are off for every event until you deliberately switch them on — so publishing an event never accidentally starts selling.

You can now switch ticket sales on yourself, from the event editor:

  1. Go to Events and open the event
  2. Set Price (USD) — what a ticket costs if you charge everyone the same
  3. Set Capacity — how many seats are for sale
  4. Tick Ticketed event
  5. Optionally set Member price and Non-member price to charge members less (see 7.4)
  6. Click Save
  7. Check the public event page — you should see the price and a booking form

On the Events screen, each card now tells you which state it is in — Ticketed or No tickets — so you can see at a glance which events are selling.

💡 Publishing and selling are separate switchesPublishing puts the event on the website. Ticketed event is what opens the till. An event can be published for weeks with sales closed, which is exactly what you want while you are still confirming a venue.
One setting your developer still handlesThe date ticket sales close is not yet in the dashboard. Everything else about pricing is now yours to set.

7.3 What Visitors See on the Events Page

Once an event is ticketed, its card on the public events page shows how many places are actually left — “12 spots available”, counting down as people buy, and “Sold out” when the last seat goes. The figure refreshes each time somebody loads the page, so it is current rather than whatever was true when the site was last published.

NoteIf the site briefly cannot reach the ticket system, the card falls back to showing the event’s full capacity rather than a live count. It corrects itself on the next page load.

7.4 Member vs Non-Member Pricing

When a member rate has been set for an event, both prices show on the event page, just as they did before. The difference is that the member rate is now verified: the site checks the buyer’s email against your member list and applies the member price only if they really are a member. On WIX, anyone could simply select the cheaper “member” ticket.

You set the two rates yourself, in the event editor:

  • Member price — what someone on your member list pays
  • Non-member price — what everyone else pays
💡 Flat-price events are fineLeave both boxes empty and the event simply sells at the single Price (USD) you entered, with one price on the page. Nothing breaks — there is just no member discount. Fill them in only for events where members should pay less.
A guard against a costly typoIf you enter a member price higher than the non-member price, the dashboard refuses to save and tells you why. That combination is almost always the two figures typed into the wrong boxes — a mistake you would otherwise discover only after a member had been overcharged.
💡 Buyers pay less than they did on WIXWIX added a 2.5% “service fee” on top of every ticket. Your site does not — a $75 ticket costs the buyer $75.

7.5 What the Buyer Receives

After paying, the buyer lands on a thank-you page carrying their “Add your guests” link, and Stripe emails them a payment receipt.

Once a seat has a name against it, that seat gets a proper ticket showing:

  • The event name, with the date, time and venue
  • The attendee — the name and email of the person actually attending
  • The ticket type — member or non-member
  • Ordered by — who bought it, and when (so a guest’s ticket shows who invited them)
  • The ticket number, order number and payment status
  • A QR code for the door
💡 Why “Ordered by” mattersWhen somebody arrives as a guest, their ticket tells you who brought them. That is the introduction you actually want to know about at the door.
⚠ The deadline for naming guestsGuests must be named by three hours after the event start time. After that the seat is forfeited — no refund, and it cannot be moved to a future event. This is deliberate: it stops unnamed seats piling up indefinitely and keeps your guest list honest. Chase unnamed seats before the event, not after.
⚠ Ticket Emails Are Not Switched On YetThe branded ticket emails — the confirmation, each guest’s own ticket, and the reminder to fill in missing names — are built and ready, but not yet switched on. They will send from your own Google mailbox, so nothing about your domain or your email has to change; your developer has a one-time setup to complete. Until then, the thank-you page is how the buyer gets their guest link. Sort this out before you sell for real.

7.6 Seeing Your Guest List

In the dashboard, the Registrations figure on the Events screen is now a real count, and each event has a guest list showing who is coming, whether they paid the member or non-member rate, and which seats still have no name against them.

💡 This is the whole pointEvery guest who gets named becomes a contact in your CRM — including people who came as somebody else’s guest. Those are exactly the warm introductions the society exists to create, so it is worth chasing the unnamed seats.

7.7 Before You Sell for Real

Four things must happen first. Your developer handles all of them:

  • Your real Stripe account connected (it is on test keys today)
  • Ticket emails switched on (a one-time setup by your developer — no change to your domain or your Google email)
  • Prices and a sales-close date set for the event
  • The old WIX listing taken down — never sell the same event in two places at once

8. Checking Guests In at the Door New

Your dashboard doubles as a door list. On the night, open it on your phone and check people in as they arrive — no printed sheet, no crossing names off.

8.1 Before the Event

  1. Sign in to the dashboard on your phone (see 3.4 to add it to your home screen)
  2. Tap Check-in and pick the event
  3. You will see the full guest list and a running count of who has arrived
💡 Do this before you leave the houseSign in while you still have good signal. The door of a golf club is a bad place to discover you have forgotten your password.

8.2 Checking Someone In

There are two ways. Scanning is quicker; typing the number always works as a backup:

  • Type the ticket number — read it off the guest’s ticket and enter it. Useful when a phone screen is cracked, dimmed, or the guest cannot find their ticket.
  • Scan their QR code — tap Start scanning and point the camera at the ticket.

When you tap Start scanning, hold the phone so the guest’s QR code fills a good part of the frame. It reads in about a second.

💡 If your phone’s built-in scanner strugglesSome phones have a built-in QR reader that quietly fails to focus on a screen. After a few seconds the dashboard notices, switches itself to a backup scanner, and tells you it has done so. You don’t need to do anything — just keep pointing at the code.

8.3 Guests With No Name Yet

If somebody arrives on a seat that was never named, the dashboard asks you for their name and email right there. Enter it and they are checked in — and they land in your CRM like any other guest.

💡 A second chance at the introductionAn unnamed seat is a person you would otherwise never have captured. Thirty seconds at the door turns them into a contact you can follow up with.

8.4 What the Screen Tells You

After each scan a full-screen result covers the camera and scanning pauses until you tap OK. That is deliberate — at a busy door it is otherwise very easy to scan three people while looking at the queue and never notice that one of them was refused.

You seeWhat it means
Green — checked inAll good. Their name and ticket type are shown.
Already checked inThis ticket has been used. It shows the time of the first check-in — useful if somebody is trying a screenshot of a friend’s ticket.
Not for this eventA valid ticket, but for a different event.
Ticket not recognisedThe number does not exist. Check for a typo first.
Refunded or expiredThe ticket is no longer valid and should not be admitted.

8.5 Patchy Signal

Golf clubs are not famous for their mobile coverage. If your connection drops mid-check-in, the dashboard holds those check-ins and sends them automatically once you are back online. Nobody gets turned away and nothing is lost.

8.6 After the Event

The arrival list stays on the event, so you can see who actually turned up as opposed to who bought a ticket. No-shows keep their record too — useful when you are deciding who to invite next time.

NoteChecking someone in never changes their ticket or their payment. It only records that they arrived.

9. Publishing — How Changes Reach the Site

You never have to press a separate "publish site" button. Saving anything in the dashboard starts the process automatically.

  1. You click Save (an event, or a page's content)
  2. The change is stored immediately — the dashboard reflects it at once
  3. An indicator reads "Publishing… live in ~30s"
  4. The website rebuilds with your change and goes live, usually within 30–60 seconds
NoteThe dashboard always shows the truth immediately. The public site takes up to a minute to catch up. If a page looks unchanged, give it a minute and refresh before worrying.
⚠ If nothing ever publishesCheck that auto-publishing is still ON in Netlify (see 2.2). With it off, changes rebuild but never go live.

10. Password Reset

  1. On the sign-in screen, click Forgot password?
  2. Enter your email address and click Send reset link
  3. Open the email and click the link — it returns you to the dashboard
  4. Type a new password (at least 8 characters) and click Save password
NoteThe link is single-use and expires after about an hour. If it's expired, simply request another.
⚠ Password Reset Is Not Working YetPassword-reset emails are not sending at the moment, so the Forgot password? link will not help you yet. This is being connected to your Google email before launch. Until it is, contact your developer to have a password reset for you — they can do it directly and safely.

Photo won't uploadCheck it's a normal image (JPG/PNG) and not enormous. Try a smaller file. If every photo fails, contact your developer.
Event won't saveLook for red text under the fields — usually a missing heading, alt text, or an invalid price. Fix the highlighted fields and save again.
Published event isn't showingConfirm the badge reads Published, then wait for the rebuild. Note that past-dated events appear under "Recent rounds" rather than "Upcoming".
Event page shows no booking formThe event is published but not ticketed. Open it in Events, tick Ticketed event, and make sure a Price is set.
Leads list is emptyCheck your search box and filters are cleared.
Reset email never arrivesPassword-reset email is not connected yet (Section 10). Contact your developer, who can reset it for you directly.
QR code won’t scan at the doorHold the phone so the code fills more of the frame and let it focus. If it still will not read, type the ticket number instead (Section 8.2).
Authenticator code rejectedWait for the next code and retype it — codes expire every 30 seconds. If every code fails, your phone’s clock may be out of sync; turn on automatic date & time.
Lost or replaced your phoneYou will not be able to complete sign-in. Contact your developer to reset your authenticator.
Check-in says "already checked in"That ticket has been used. The time of the first check-in is shown — if that was not them, they may be showing somebody else’s ticket.
Site is completely downCheck Netlify for a failed deploy, then contact your developer.
